1. The Crisis Is Now

Click to expand: Why SAI testing is urgent...

2024 was the hottest year in recorded history. Marine heatwaves are killing coral reefs in real-time. Antarctic sea ice hit record lows for the third consecutive year. Extreme weather events are intensifying faster than models predicted.

"Net zero by 2050" is not a solution to a 2024 problem. Even aggressive decarbonization cannot reverse warming already locked in. Active cooling is no longer optional—it is necessary.

The gap between climate rhetoric and climate reality has become undeniable. Global CO₂ emissions continue rising. The 1.5°C target is functionally dead. We are on track for 2.5-3°C by 2100 under current policies.

SAI is not a substitute for decarbonization. It is a necessary complement—a tourniquet while we address the underlying injury.

Willful Blindness

Click to expand: The disconnect between rhetoric and action...

Scientists hedge with "by end of century" framing while disasters unfold now. Policy discussions focus on mitigation pathways while adaptation fails. The word "emergency" is used but not acted upon.

The climate science community has been socialized into caution. Careers are built on careful, qualified statements. But this professional conservatism has become maladaptive when the house is on fire.

Consider the disconnect: We acknowledge we're in a "climate emergency" but treat SAI research as something that might be appropriate "in the future, if things get bad enough." Things are bad enough. The emergency is now.

This is not a call for recklessness. It's a call for appropriate urgency. Medical ethics allows experimental treatments for terminal patients. The planet is the patient.

The Distortion of SAI

Click to expand: How SAI has been mischaracterized...

SAI research has been systematically mischaracterized:

  • "Playing God" — We're already doing that with 40 billion tons of CO₂/year
  • "Unknown risks" — Volcanoes have run the experiment; we know more than critics admit
  • "Moral hazard" — The real moral hazard is refusing to research options while the crisis accelerates

The de facto moratorium on outdoor SAI testing is ideological, not scientific. In 30 years, not one credible scientific objection has prevented a single small-scale test from generating useful data.

The Catch-22

Click for details on the circular logic blocking research...

The objections follow a pattern:

  1. Models raise concerns about SAI
  2. Testing is forbidden until concerns are resolved
  3. Concerns cannot be resolved without data from tests
  4. Return to step 1

This is not science. It is a Catch-22 designed to prevent research while maintaining plausible deniability.

Responses to Common Objections

Click for detailed rebuttals...

"Termination shock"

If SAI stops suddenly, warming resumes. This is true—and it's an argument for careful planning, not an argument against research. SAI's reversibility is a safety feature, not a bug.

"Ozone depletion"

A legitimate concern that requires data to quantify. Models disagree. Only measurement can resolve this.

"Governance challenges"

Real, but not addressed by refusing to develop the technology. We don't ban medical research because healthcare governance is imperfect.

"Unilateral deployment risk"

The greater risk is that desperation drives deployment without adequate research. The way to prevent reckless deployment is responsible testing now.

FAST 2025 is designed to break the 30-year taboo on even minimal SAI testing. The alternative is permanent paralysis.

2. Why Antarctica?

Click to expand: Strategic advantages of Antarctic testing...

FAST is designed to be the least objectionable possible SAI test:

  • Location: Antarctica — minimal population, existing research infrastructure, contained polar vortex
  • Scale: Starting at tons, not megatons — detectable but not climate-altering
  • Duration: Pulse injection, not sustained — effects dissipate in 1-2 years
  • Transparency: All data public, all methods documented, international participation invited

Detailed Advantages

Scientific, political, and practical benefits...

Scientific advantages

The polar vortex provides natural containment during austral winter. Stratospheric conditions differ from tropics (where most models focus), providing novel data. Existing research stations enable logistics.

Political advantages

No nation's agriculture or monsoons at risk. Minimal population exposure concerns. Precedent of international scientific cooperation (Antarctic Treaty).

Practical advantages

C-17 aircraft already operate from McMurdo. Balloon launches routine. Infrastructure exists.

This is not geoengineering. This is atmospheric science with policy relevance.

Logical Trajectory

Phased approach with decision gates...

Phase 1 — Validation (Tiers 0-2): Do we understand the basic physics and chemistry? Can we measure what we inject?

Phase 2 — Quantification (Tiers 2-3): What are the actual ozone impacts? Can we detect radiative effects?

Phase 3 — Decision Support (Tier 4): Does the data support operational deployment? What are the real risks and benefits?

Each phase generates go/no-go criteria for the next. This is not a commitment to deployment—it's a commitment to learning.

Off-ramps built in: "Stop and revise" is not failure—it's the scientific method working correctly. The only true failure is refusing to generate data.

Aerosol Selection

Click to expand: SO₂ vs Calcite comparison...

The SO₂ problem: Sulfur dioxide has been the default SAI aerosol for decades. But "sulfur" evokes acid rain, pollution, industrial contamination. The optics are terrible, regardless of the science. This is not a trivial concern—public perception shapes political feasibility.

Tier 0: Dual-Aerosol Comparison

  • Aircraft carry segregated containers: half SO₂, half calcite
  • Flight trajectories inject SO₂ load first, calcite load second at different locations
  • Identical sensor coverage for both plumes
  • Head-to-head empirical comparison under identical conditions

Calcite: Pros and Cons

Advantages and uncertainties of calcium carbonate...

Calcite advantages:

  • Public perception: "Calcium carbonate" is chalk, limestone, antacids—benign associations
  • Potential ozone benefits: Some models suggest calcite may neutralize ozone-depleting chlorine chemistry rather than exacerbating it
  • No sulfuric acid formation: Avoids stratospheric H₂SO₄ entirely

Calcite uncertainties:

  • Less natural proof-of-concept (no "calcite volcanoes")
  • Particle size control may differ from SO₂→H₂SO₄ pathway
  • Supply chain for stratospheric-grade calcite less established

Tiers 1-4: Aerosol choice pending Tier 0 results. If calcite performs comparably or better, it becomes the default. Data decides—not ideology, not aesthetics.

Sensor Network: "Sensor Gas"

Click to expand: Distributed sensor approach...

Traditional atmospheric science uses a few expensive instruments. FAST inverts this:

"Sensor gas": Hundreds to thousands of cheap, lightweight sensor packages distributed through the atmosphere. Trade individual precision for statistical power and spatial coverage.

  • Tracer nodes: GPS + satellite modem only, 150g, ~$300 each
  • Sensor nodes: + particle counter + IMU, 500g, ~$8,400 each
  • Specialty nodes: SO₂ sensors, radiometers, chemistry packages — higher cost, fewer units

Communications: Redundant systems—LoRa mesh networking for local data relay, Iridium satellite for global uplink.

Why This Approach?

Redundancy through numbers...

If 30% of nodes fail, the network still works. Redundancy through numbers, not through expensive engineering.

The sensor gas concept also serves as a hardware validation platform. Consumer-grade air quality sensors (~$20) are untested at stratospheric conditions (-40°C to -80°C, low pressure). FAST can characterize which cheap sensors survive and perform, potentially transforming the economics of future atmospheric monitoring.

Design philosophy: Mass-producibility and fail-tolerance.

Injection Methods

Click to expand: Dispersal techniques by tier...

Tier 0: Dual-Aerosol Cube Dispersal

Tier 0 deploys both SO₂ and calcite using identical dispersal mechanisms for direct comparison:

  • Aircraft carry segregated cargo: SO₂ cubes forward, calcite cubes aft (or separate flights)
  • Powdered material packaged in 1 m³ cubes
  • Cubes expelled from C-17 cargo door via mechanized rail system
  • Small charges embedded in cubes detonate after safe separation from aircraft, dispersing powder
  • SO₂ and calcite injected at different locations along flight trajectory

Later Tier Methods

Evolution of dispersal techniques...

Tier 1-2: Refined cube dispersal or pressurized release

Depending on Tier 0 results:

  • If cube dispersal performs well: continue with selected aerosol
  • Option to test pressurized gas release (SO₂) or slurry spray (calcite)
  • Particle size distribution measurements inform method selection

Tier 3-4: Optimized dispersal systems

  • Engineered nozzles for tighter particle size distribution control
  • Method selected based on empirical results from earlier tiers
  • Optimized for aerosol formation efficiency and radiative effectiveness

Testing multiple methods empirically rather than assuming one is optimal.

Supply Chain Status

Click to expand: Logistics and procurement...

Key supply chain elements for FAST:

SO₂ sourcing: Industrial commodity, ~$300/ton. 40 kilotons = ~$12M in materials. Not a bottleneck.

Aircraft: C-17 or equivalent heavy-lift aircraft. Available via military or commercial charter. Limiting factor is Antarctic runway availability during injection windows.

Balloons: Latex weather balloons are commodity items. Superpressure long-duration balloons more specialized but available (CNES, NASA heritage designs).

Sensors: Mixed readiness. Particle counters available commercially. Electrochemical SO₂ sensors need cold-weather validation. Optical instruments available but expensive. IMUs are commodity (MEMS accelerometers).

Calcite Supply Chain

Additional considerations for calcium carbonate...

Bulk calcium carbonate: Cheap (~$150/ton), widely available.

Stratospheric-grade material: Requires controlled particle size distribution, may need specialized milling and quality control. Estimated $500-1000/ton.

Lead time: Less established supply chain than SO₂. Would need to identify/qualify vendors for large-scale procurement.

4. Adaptive Timeline

Click to expand: Decision points and criteria...

Guiding principle: Maximum safe speed. Delay in proceeding with tests must be minimized.

Tier 0: Target 2026, contingent on funding.

All subsequent tiers: Proceed as soon as prior tier results are analyzed and necessary changes implemented. No arbitrary waiting periods. No "pause for reflection." If the data supports proceeding, proceed.

The climate crisis does not wait. Neither should we.

Decision Points:

  • Tier 0 → 1: Did equipment survive? Did data link work? Were balloons recoverable?
  • Tier 1 → 2: Did we detect enhanced aerosol? Did dispersion match models?
  • Tier 2 → 3: Did particle size distribution match predictions? Any unexpected chemistry?
  • Tier 3 → 4: Did radiative measurements show expected signal? What were ozone impacts?

Off-Ramp Criteria

When to stop or revise...

  • If Tier 1 shows SO₂ doesn't convert to aerosol as expected → stop and revise theory
  • If Tier 2 shows unexpected chemistry → stop and investigate
  • If Tier 3 shows ozone depletion worse than models predict → stop and reconsider
  • If Tier 4 shows cooling less than expected → revise efficiency assumptions

"Stop and revise" is not failure—it's the scientific method working correctly.

Research Principles & Funding

Click to expand: Transparency and funding approach...

FAST operates under explicit principles:

  • Transparency: All protocols pre-registered. All data publicly released. No proprietary claims.
  • Open participation: International collaborators invited. Methods fully documented.
  • Research intent only: Explicit prohibition on deployment intent. This is science, not implementation.
  • Safe materials: SO₂ baseline; exploring alternatives with potential safety/perception advantages.
  • Separation: Strict separation from proprietary geoengineering ventures. FAST is public science.

Funding Landscape

Who could fund this and why they haven't...

US Government: Has the aircraft (230 C-17s), the Antarctic infrastructure (McMurdo), and the budget. Lacks political will. Any administration funding SAI research faces attacks from both environmentalists and climate deniers.

Other governments: Similar constraints. No nation wants to be "the country that geoengineered."

Philanthropists: Several billionaires have expressed interest in climate intervention. Advantages: speed, independence. Disadvantages: "billionaire playing God" narrative, limited long-term commitment.

Research institutions: Could lead scientific design, unlikely to fund deployment at scale.

Recommendation: Hybrid model. Philanthropic funding for Tiers 0-2 (~$26M proof of concept). Government involvement at Tier 3+ once political cover exists from successful early tests.

Breaking the Deadlock

The current equilibrium and how to escape it...

The path forward requires breaking the current equilibrium where:

  • Researchers won't propose tests because funders won't fund them
  • Funders won't fund tests because researchers don't propose them
  • Everyone waits for someone else to go first

FAST is a concrete proposal with specific costs, specific measurements, and specific decision criteria. It can be critiqued, revised, and improved. What it cannot do is generate data while remaining a thought experiment.

The question is not whether SAI research is risky.
The question is whether the risks of ignorance exceed the risks of knowledge.
